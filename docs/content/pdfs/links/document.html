Links
to a page.

A link to a page takes the reader to the exact spot it names, without leaving the document.

Long documents are rarely read front to back. A contents page, like the one on page 1, lets a reader pick a chapter and go straight there.

Cross-references do the same inside the text. The table of web links is on page 4; the page that “Go to page 3” opens is page 3.

Footnotes are links too. The number after this sentence jumps to its note at the foot of the page.1

The pointer turns into a hand over every link, so a reader always knows what will respond to a click.

# Screenshot protection

For a document people may read but must not capture, the viewer can show its pages as protected
video, the way streaming services show films. A screenshot, a screen recording or a shared screen
then shows the page black, while it stays sharp on the reader's own screen.

Everything on top of the page works as always: selecting text, search, links, comments and other
annotations, form fields.

## Turn it on

It's a permission, so it's per user. Give the user's [token](/docs/engine/authentication) or
grant `protected-display.view`, and leave out `doc.render`:

```js
const scope = [
  'doc.open',
  'protected-display.view',
  'doc.text.select',
  'doc.text.search',
  'doc.annotate.read',
  'doc.forms.read',
];
```

Without `doc.render` the server sends no page pictures at all, and with `protected-display.view` it
sends protected video frames instead. The viewer sees both and switches by itself: there's nothing
to set in the viewer. A user who may render pages sees them the usual way.

Leave out what would let the page out another way too: `doc.render.thumbnail` (the thumbnails are
plain pictures), `doc.download`, `doc.print` and `doc.text.copy`, unless this user may have them.

Your CloudPDF server needs the protected display module, with a DRM account to issue the licenses.
See [Server modules](/docs/server/configuration/modules).

## What the reader sees

- **One page at a time.** Scrolling through every page at once would take a video player per
  page. The page layout options show turned off, with a sentence that says why.
- **Sharp at every zoom, up to 500%.** Up to about twice the size that fits the screen, the page
  is one video frame. Zoom in further and stop: a moment later, a sharp frame of just the part on
  screen replaces it. Moving shows the whole page again at once. A video is drawn as one piece
  the size it shows, and past 500% a page's is more than many computers' graphics can hold.
- **A white page for a moment** when a page first shows, while its frame arrives.

## Browsers

The viewer picks the protection the browser has:

| Browser | Protection | A screenshot shows |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Chrome, on Mac, Windows and Linux | Widevine | Black |
| Edge, on Windows and Mac | Widevine | Black |
| Firefox, on Mac and Windows | Widevine | Black |
| Safari, on Mac, iPhone and iPad | FairPlay | Black |

Rounded corners, transparency and effects on the page are left off: they would take the video off
the screen's protected layer.

## What it doesn't stop

A camera pointed at the screen. And what the user may do otherwise: someone who may print or
download the document, or copy its text, can take it out that way.

## In a viewer you build yourself

The CloudPDF viewer includes it. With the headless viewer, add the plugin with the browser's
player:

```ts
import { protectedDisplayPlugin } from '@cloudpdf/plugin-protected-display';

const plugins = [
  /* … */
  protectedDisplayPlugin({
    player: () => import('@cloudpdf/web').then((web) => web.browserProtectedPlayer()),
  }),
];
```

The plugin decides what shows and speaks to your server; the player plays the frames. As a
function, the player is imported only for a protected user. The plugin needs the render and stage
plugins, and does nothing on the local engine or for a user who may render pages. Without a
player it stays off, and a protected user sees no pages.

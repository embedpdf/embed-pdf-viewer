# Single pages & thumbnails

`<PageView>` shows one page on its own, without scrolling or zoom: a preview in a card, a page
picker in a dialog, the page a comment belongs to. Give it a page and a width:

<Snippet name="viewing/page-preview" />

Every layer works in `<PageView>` as it does on the Stage: selection, annotations, search
highlights. So does a menu anchored to the page. The page keeps its rotation and redraws when it
changes.

## Pick the page

`page` takes a page's `ref` or its index, so `0` is the first page:

<Snippet name="viewing/first-page" />

`usePageList()` gives you every page of the document, in order, to loop over:

<Snippet name="viewing/page-picker" />

A page's `label` is the page number the PDF defines, such as `'iv'` or `'A-2'`, when it has one.

## A thumbnail strip

For a strip next to the main view, with many pages that scroll, use a second Stage rather than many
`<PageView>`s: it mounts only the thumbnails you can see, and it can follow the page the reader is on.

<Snippet name="viewing/thumbnail-strip-token" />

<Snippet name="viewing/thumbnail-strip" />

- **Turning off `interaction`** in the strip's settings keeps a drag on it from selecting text in
  the document.
- **Turning off `zoomGestures`** keeps the thumbnails their size under a pinch.
- **`reveal()`** scrolls the strip only as far as needed, so it follows without jumping.

See [Two views of one document](stage#two-views-of-one-document) for everything a second Stage can
do.

## Props

| Prop         | What it is                                                              | Default          |
| :----------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------- |
| `page`       | The page, as its `ref` or its index                                     | required         |
| `width`      | The width of the page, in pixels                                        | `240`            |
| `documentId` | Which document                                                          | the active one   |
| `fallback`   | What to show until the page is ready                                    | nothing          |
| `pageFrame`  | Space reserved around the page for your own labels, in pixels per side  | `0`              |
| `pageChrome` | What to draw in that space                                              | —                |

<Fw only="react">

`fallback` and `pageChrome` take content, and `className` and `style` go on the outer box.

</Fw>

<Fw only="vue">

`fallback` and `page-chrome` are slots, `<template #fallback>` and `<template #page-chrome>`, and
`class` and `style` go on the outer box.

</Fw>

<Fw only="svelte">

`fallback` and `pageChrome` are snippets, and `class` and `style` go on the outer box.

</Fw>

<Fw only="angular">

`fallback` and `pageChrome` are templates, `<ng-template epdfFallback>` and
`<ng-template epdfPageChrome>`, and `class` and `style` go on the outer box.

</Fw>

## Good to know

### How pages look

A page here has the same background and shadow as on the Stage: the viewer's `page` settings, or
`--epdf-page-background` and `--epdf-page-shadow` from CSS (see [Colors & theming](../ui/theming)).

### Menus inside a page view

A menu anchored to the page, such as the selection menu, is placed above everything else on the
page, so a card or a scrolling list can't cut it off.

### Pointer input

A `<PageView>` takes pointer input for the tools when the interaction plugin is registered, so text
selection and annotation editing work in it too. On a touch screen, the Stage is the better choice:
it has the gestures.

# Your own annotation look

The annotation layer draws each annotation the way the PDF describes it. You can draw some of them
with your own <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> instead: a note that looks like your app's comment bubble, a stamp
that shows live data from your server.

This changes the screen only. The PDF keeps the annotation's own look, so other PDF apps,
printing and downloads show the original:

| Where                               | What people see                 |
| :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| Your viewer                         | Your <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> |
| The saved PDF, other apps, printing | The annotation's own appearance |

## Its look, or UI on it

Ask whether it should zoom and turn with the PDF:

| You want | Use | It |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A different look: a comment bubble instead of the note icon, a stamp with live data | A [renderer](#draw-an-annotation-your-way) | Is part of the page: it zooms and turns with it, and sits under the selection handles |
| A mark that stays on the annotation: a ✓, a status, a count of replies | [Pinned anchored UI](#pin-ui-to-an-annotation) | Floats over the pages, upright and the same size at every zoom, exactly where you put it |
| A card or a menu next to it | [Anchored UI](#show-a-card-on-hover) | Floats over the pages, and moves to stay in view |

<Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>`for`</Fw><Fw only="angular">The function you give `epdfAnnotation`</Fw>
gets the annotation, with all its fields, so you can pick by kind, name, author or anything else.<Fw only="angular">
A subtype picks every annotation of that kind: `epdfAnnotation="stamp"`.</Fw> The first renderer that

<Fw only="react">

Define the list once, outside your component or in `useMemo`. The layer registers each entry, and
a new list every render would register them again (the viewer warns about it while you develop).

</Fw>

Your <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> draws into a **frame**: a box the layer places, turns and scales exactly like
the annotation's own drawing, also while it's dragged, resized or turned. Fill it, with `width` and
`height` at `100%`, and draw at the annotation's 100% size: the layer scales your drawing with the
page, text and borders included, as it does the PDF's own:

<Snippet name="annotations/custom-rendering/bubble" />

The selection outline, the click area and the hover all use the frame, so a look that fills it
matches them. A note keeps its size on screen as people zoom in and stays upright when the page is
turned, and so does a look that fills its frame. To make the bubble bigger, make the note bigger: a
size in points is a third more in pixels at 100% zoom, so a 24-point note is 32 pixels.

<Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>Your component gets:</Fw><Fw only="angular">Your template gets these:
the annotation is `let-annotation`, and the rest are named, such as `let-frame="frame"`.</Fw>

| Prop          | What it is                                                                          |
| :------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `annotation`  | The annotation, as `get()` returns it.                                              |
| `frame`       | The box you draw into: its `width` and `height` in pixels at the annotation's 100% size, its `rotation` on screen in degrees clockwise, and the `scale` the layer draws it at. Turn your content by `-rotation` to keep it upright, such as a label on a turned stamp. |
| `native`      | The layer's own drawing of it, filling the frame. Render it to keep the original look and add to it. |
| `appearance`  | The engine's picture of the annotation as an image (`url`), or `null`. `native` already draws it in place. |
| `hovered`     | True while the pointer is over it.                                                  |
| `selected`    | True while it's selected, such as to make room for the selection handles.           |
| `interactive` | True when what you draw takes the pointer (see [below](#take-over-the-pointer)).    |

## Pin UI to an annotation

A ✓ on an approved stamp, a status under it, a count of replies next to a note: UI that belongs to
one annotation and stays on it. `pinned` keeps it exactly where you put it. It doesn't flip or move to
stay in view, and it scrolls away with the annotation:

<Snippet name="annotations/custom-rendering/pinned" />

<Fw only="react">Mount one for each annotation that needs it in the Stage's `overlay`</Fw><Fw only="vue">Mount one for each annotation that needs it in the Stage's `#overlay` slot</Fw><Fw only="svelte">Mount one for each annotation that needs it in the Stage's `overlay` snippet</Fw><Fw only="angular">Put one for each annotation that needs it inside `<epdf-stage>`</Fw>,
from `useAnnotationList()`. Anchored UI on a page that isn't on screen isn't drawn, so a document
with hundreds of them costs only the ones in view.

<Example name="annotation/stamp-status" />

> **Try it:** sign the second stamp off, then select a stamp and move it: its status goes with it. The
> second stamp's status hangs over the next page, and stays on top.

1. The stamps are placed like any stamp, so the PDF holds the real ones; see [Stamps](stamps).
2. Each one is picked out by its `name`, which the stamp keeps in every PDF app.
3. The status sits under the stamp with `placement="bottom"`, and the ✓ on its corner with
   `placement="top-end"` and a negative `gap`. See [Where it goes](../ui/anchored#where-it-goes).
4. The stamp keeps its own look and stays selectable: the buttons are next to it, not in it.

To find your own data for an annotation, key it by `annotationKey(annotation.ref)`, which stays the
same after the document is saved and opened again, or by its `name` when you gave it one.

## Good to know

### Drawing at the size on screen

A renderer with `scale: false` draws at the frame's size on screen instead, and sizes its text and
borders itself from `frame.scale`. Use it for something that must lay itself out at full resolution,
such as a canvas.

### Hover cards people can use

A card closes when the pointer leaves the annotation, so the pointer can't reach a button in it.
Keep the card open while the pointer is over the card itself, for example with a short delay
before it closes and a `pointerenter` on the card that cancels it.

### Form fields

A form field's widgets aren't annotations: they're the form's, and the `<FormLayer>` draws them, so
your renderers never get them. See [Forms and annotations](../forms#forms-and-annotations).

### Other layers

A renderer replaces the drawing inside the annotation layer. Anything that isn't tied to one
annotation, such as a comments sidebar, belongs in your own UI, fed by `useAnnotationList()`.

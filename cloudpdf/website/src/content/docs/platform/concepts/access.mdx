# Grants and tokens

Who opens the document decides how they get in. Ask one question: does your app know who they are?

- **No: anyone may open it.** A reader of your website, a client you send a link, a visitor scanning
  a QR code. Make a [grant](/docs/platform/grants) in the dashboard.
- **Yes: they're signed in to your app.** Your server knows who they are and what they may see.
  Give them a [token](/docs/platform/app/tokens).

| | A grant | A token |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Who opens it | Anyone with the link, or on your website | One of your signed-in users |
| Made by | You, in the dashboard (or your server) | Your server, with an API key, for each user |
| Says who they are | No: everyone is a visitor, who may answer a few questions | Yes: your user's id and name |
| Lasts | Until you pause, change or end it | Minutes or hours, then your server makes the next |
| You change it by | Editing it: every link and embed follows | Making the next token differently |
| Needs a server | No | Yes: the route that makes tokens |

## Both end the same way

Neither a grant nor a token is what the viewer keeps. When a document opens, CloudPDF gives the
viewer a short pass for that one document, with the permissions you chose, and checks every page,
search and change against it:

- **With a grant**, the viewer trades the grant for the pass, and trades again before the pass runs
  out. Change the grant, and the next trade follows it.
- **With a token**, the token is the pass. The viewer asks your server for the next one before it
  runs out.

So permissions read the same everywhere: a grant's **Comment** and a token's `permissions.comment`
are the same thing.

## One document, both ways

A document can have grants and tokens at the same time. The brochure on your website opens with a
grant, and your sales team opens the same file in your app with tokens, with their own names on
their comments. Ending the grant doesn't touch the tokens, and the other way round.

## Visitors and users

A grant's visitors are people CloudPDF remembers, but doesn't know. Their browser keeps who they are
between visits, so what they add stays theirs, and a grant can ask their name or email before they
start. [Visitors](/docs/platform/grants/visitors) shows who came.

Your app's users are people you know. The token carries their id and name, so everything they add
says who added it, in your words.

# Building forms

The form plugin can also add fields to a PDF, and change or remove the ones it has, so people can turn
any document into a form. Switch to **design mode**, pick a field tool, and click or drag on the page:

<Snippet name="forms/field-palette" />

Building needs the [annotation plugin](../annotations) next to the form plugin: in design mode, fields
are boxes you select, move, resize and style like any annotation.

| Tool             | Places                      | A click makes |
| :--------------- | :-------------------------- | :------------ |
| `form-text`      | A text box                  | 160 × 24      |
| `form-checkbox`  | A checkbox                  | 18 × 18       |
| `form-radio`     | A radio button              | 18 × 18       |
| `form-combobox`  | A dropdown                  | 140 × 24      |
| `form-listbox`   | A list                      | 140 × 72      |
| `form-button`    | A push button               | 100 × 28      |
| `form-signature` | A field someone signs later | 160 × 48      |

A see-through preview of the field follows the pointer, drawn as the field will be, so people see
where it lands before they click. Drag instead of clicking for another size. The tool stays active, so placing ten checkboxes is ten
clicks.

### On a turned page

On a page that is shown turned, such as a scan saved sideways, a field is placed the way its
author sees the page: a click makes the tool's size as the page shows it, and the field is turned
so that what's typed into it reads upright there. The turn is saved with the field as a quarter
turn, the one turn a PDF form field can hold, so every PDF app shows it and fills it in the same
way. `upright: false` on a tool places its fields along the page instead; see
[Rotated pages](../annotations/tools#rotated-pages).

Turn the page, not the view, before you place fields: a page turned with
[`pageEdit.rotateBy()`](../documents/pages#rotate-pages) is saved with the document, and a view rotation is this
session's only. Turn pages before the first signature too, since turning a page afterwards is a
change to a signed document.

## Design mode

The `form-edit` tool is design mode: fields stop being fillable and become boxes you can select, move
and resize. The `pointer` tool makes them fillable again:

```ts
interaction.activateTool('form-edit'); // design the form
interaction.activateTool('pointer'); // fill it in
```

In design mode the form lends its boxes to the annotation plugin, which selects, moves, resizes,
restyles, reorders and deletes them with its own tools, next to the annotations. Every edit of a box
is still the form's: its place or look is `updateWidget()`, and deleting every box of a field
deletes the field. A drag of two fields and a stamp is one change: one request, and one step to
undo. Like every change, it shows at once and the engine's answer follows.

A field just placed is selected at once, before the engine answers, and the tool goes back to
`form-edit` rather than the default pointer, so design mode goes on. Without `canDesign()` nothing is
lent: the fields stay fillable boxes the annotation tools leave alone.

## Place fields for a signer

When a form goes to several people, each field belongs to one of them: it's in their
[group](/docs/engine/forms/groups). Tell the document
[who its groups are](../forms#fields-for-several-people), then say who the next fields are for:

```ts
form.setDesignGroup('buyer'); // fields placed with a tool are Dana's
```

<Snippet name="forms/signing-flow/sender-toolbar" />

While you design, every field shows in its group's color. A field placed with a tool goes into
the group you set, which the form's state has as <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>`designGroup`</Fw><Fw only="angular">`form.designGroup()`</Fw>. With `null`, where it starts, it names
no group, and a placed field is in none.

Putting a field in a group other than your own takes the `fields:set-group` permission for that
group, and `form.canSetGroup(groupId)` says whether the user has it. Turn off the signers a user
can't place for: `setDesignGroup()` refuses a group they may not put a field in, as `create()`,
`update()` and `moveToGroup()` do. A field in no group needs no such permission: whoever may
design the form may leave a field in none, or take it out of its group.

### Give fields to a signer

A form made somewhere else, in Acrobat for example, arrives with its fields in no group. Move one
like any setting, and `null` takes it out of its group again:

```ts
await form.update(field.ref, { groupId: 'seller' });
await form.update(field.ref, { groupId: null }); // nobody's
```

Or move every selected field in one call, which is one step to undo:

<Snippet name="forms/signing-flow/move-selected" />

<Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>`selectedFields`</Fw><Fw only="angular">`form.selectedFields()`</Fw> in the form's state has every field with a selected box, and `form.listSelectedFields()`
reads it once. `moveToGroup(fields, groupId)` leaves out the fields already in the group, shows
the rest in their new color at once, and resolves `{ fields }` as they are now. With `null` it
takes the fields out of their group.

`update()` takes only what you change, with the same settings a field reads:

| Setting                                      | What it is                                            |
| :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- |
| `name`                                       | Its own part of its name: renaming `customer.email` to `phone` makes `customer.phone` |
| `required`                                   | PDF apps ask for a value before the form is submitted |
| `readOnly`                                   | People can't change the value                         |
| `alternateName`                              | The tooltip, also read by screen readers              |
| `defaultValue`                               | What a reset puts back                                |
| `options`                                    | For a dropdown or list: `{ label, value }` pairs      |
| `maxLength`, `multiline`, `password`, `comb` | For a text box                                        |
| `groupId`                                    | The group it's in: who fills it in. [Give fields to a signer](#give-fields-to-a-signer) |

`getFieldSettings(ref)` describes a field's settings for a panel you draw without knowing the
field's kind: `{ properties, values }`, each property a `key` (what `update()` takes), a `control`
(`text`, `flag`, `number`, `choice`, `choices` or `options`), and a `label` in English with its
translation key, `labelKey` (`form.settings.tooltip`), in display order. A `choice`'s options are
the field's own values, shown as they are. While the document has groups, `groupId` is one of the
settings: a `choice` whose options are the ids of the groups the user may put the field in. Show
each with its group's label, from the form's `groups`; none of them, `null`, takes the field out
of its group.

A field's look, such as its border, background and font, belongs to its box: change it like any
annotation's, with the [style panel](../annotations/selecting#a-style-panel), or from code with
`form.updateWidget(widget.ref, { color: '#1d4ed8' })`.

## Turn a field

A field turns by quarter turns, in two ways:

```ts
// The whole box: it stands the other way, and its contents turn with it
await annotation.selection.rotateBy(90); // -90 the other way; resetRotation() for upright

// Only its contents: the box stays where it stands, as Acrobat's Orientation does
await annotation.selection.update((widget) => orientationPatch(widget, 90));
```

`rotateBy()` turns the selected fields as it turns a stamp, about each box's middle (several
together turn about their middle). A field near the edge of its page stays on the page: where the
turn would carry it past the edge, it lands against the edge. A field has no rotation handle,
because a quarter turn is all it can hold. `orientationPatch()` comes with the annotation plugin:
it gives the `box` and `rotation` that turn a field's contents where it stands.

The [style panel](../annotations/selecting#a-style-panel) lists the second as `orientation` for a
selection of fields: a choice of `'0'`, `'90'`, `'180'` and `'270'`, clockwise. Without a
selection, `form.updateWidget(widget.ref, orientationPatch(widget, 90))` does the same for one
box, and `form.updateWidget(widget.ref, { rotation: 90 })` turns the whole box. A box reads its
turn as `rotation`, with `box`, the frame its contents are laid out in, and `rect`, where it
stands; see [Turned fields](../forms#turned-fields).

## Add fields from code

`create()` takes the same settings the engine takes, with the page and the box for each place the
field shows:

```ts
const { field } = await form.create({
  kind: 'combobox',
  name: 'country',
  options: [
    { label: 'Netherlands', value: 'NL' },
    { label: 'Belgium', value: 'BE' },
  ],
  widgets: [{ page: page.ref, rect: { x: 72, y: 252, width: 148, height: 20 } }],
});
```

A radio group is one field with a box per button, each with the value it stands for:

```ts
await form.create({
  kind: 'radio',
  name: 'plan',
  widgets: [
    { page: page.ref, rect: { x: 72, y: 136, width: 16, height: 16 }, exportValue: 'monthly' },
    { page: page.ref, rect: { x: 72, y: 166, width: 16, height: 16 }, exportValue: 'yearly' },
  ],
});
```

A field placed with a tool gets a name like `text_1`; rename it in your settings panel. Every
kind's settings are on [Creating form fields](/docs/engine/forms/authoring).

### One more button for a group

`addWidget(ref)` shows a field in one more place: for a radio group a new choice, for a checkbox a
box that checks together with the others.

```ts
const { widget } = await form.addWidget(plan.ref);
annotation.selection.select([widget.ref]); // in design mode, so it can be dragged
```

Without a place, it goes next in line: below the last button if they stand in a column, beside it
if in a row, at the same size and the same spacing, starting a new column where the page ends. It
looks like the field's last button, and a radio group's new choice gets the next free `Choice<n>`.
Pass what you want instead: `form.addWidget(plan.ref, { rect, exportValue: 'yearly' })` takes
everything an entry of `create()`'s `widgets` takes.

## Copy fields

`duplicateWidgets()` copies boxes, as Duplicate does in a designer. What a copy becomes follows
from what its box is:

- **A radio button is one answer to a question.** Its copy is another button in the same group,
  with the next free `Choice<n>` as its value.
- **Any other box is a whole question.** Its copy is a new field, named after the original:
  `email` becomes `email_2`.

```ts
// another button in the group, below the one it copies
await form.duplicateWidgets([button.ref], {
  offset: { x: 0, y: 24 },
});

// a new text field beside the original
const { fields } = await form.duplicateWidgets([box.ref], {
  offset: { x: 10, y: 10 },
});
```

Boxes of one field that are copied together become one new field. `duplicate()` copies whole
fields instead: a radio group becomes a new group with the same buttons and the same values.

```ts
const { fields } = await form.duplicate([plan.ref], {
  offset: { x: 0, y: 80 },
});
```

A new field has its original's settings, scripts and default value. It doesn't have its answer:
it starts empty, as a new field does. Pass `values: true` to copy the answer too. A signature
never comes along.

| Option   | What it does                                              | Default            |
| :------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------- |
| `page`   | The page the copies go on, as a ref or an index.          | each on its own    |
| `offset` | How far each copy moves: `{ x, y }` in page points.       | `{ x: 0, y: 0 }`   |
| `values` | Whether a new field gets its original's answer.           | `false`            |

Both resolve `{ fields, widgets, dropped }`: the fields that were made or got a new button, the
new boxes, and what was left out. A copy leaves out what
an [import](/docs/engine/forms/import-export#whats-left-out) leaves out: a field of a kind the
engine doesn't know, a script when `canWriteScripts()` is false, an action it can't write. A box
in no field is left out too.

One call makes at most 10,000 new fields: the engine's item
[limit](/docs/engine/forms/import-export#limits), which a server can change. More is refused with
`invalid-input`, and nothing is copied.

In design mode, [Duplicate the selection](../annotations/selecting#duplicate-the-selection)
does this for the selected boxes, together with the annotations selected beside them.

## Scripts and calculations

A field's scripts go in `actions` when you create or change it: a calculated total, a formatted
amount, a check on what the user typed. The viewer runs them while the user fills the form in.

```ts
await form.update(total.ref, {
  actions: {
    calculate: {
      type: 'javascript',
      script: 'event.value = this.getField("subtotal").value * 1.21;',
    },
  },
});
await form.reorderCalculations([tax.ref], { before: total.ref });
```

A calculate script puts the field at the end of the order calculations run in;
`reorderCalculations()` moves it. Writing a script needs `canWriteScripts()`. See
[Scripts and calculations](/docs/engine/forms/authoring#scripts-and-calculations).

A box can do something when it's clicked, too, such as reset the form: give its entry of
`widgets` in `create()` its `actions`. A script, a submit or a web link there needs
`canWriteScripts()` as well. See [Widget actions](/docs/engine/forms/authoring#widget-actions).
A push button (`kind: 'pushbutton'`) is a box that only does that, with a `caption`; see
[A push button](/docs/engine/forms/authoring#a-push-button).

## Remove a field

```ts
await form.delete(field.ref);
```

This removes the field and every box it has. To remove one box of a field that shows in several places,
such as a reference number on every page, use `form.deleteWidget(widget.ref)`. It resolves the field
as it is now. In design mode, deleting the selection does the same: one box goes from its field, and
a field whose every box is selected goes whole.

Every building method shows its change at once, is one step to undo, and resolves once the engine
answered.

## Good to know

### Fields and boxes

A field is the data: its name, its value, its settings. A **widget** is a box where the field shows on
a page. Most fields have one; a radio group has one per button, and a field can show on several pages,
all with the same value.

### Signature fields

A signature field is a place for someone to sign later. Signing it is on [Signatures](signatures).

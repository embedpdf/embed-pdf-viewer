# How it works

The viewer is one HTML element, `<embedpdf-viewer>`, with the whole interface inside it. The
framework packages are thin wrappers that render that element, so the viewer is the same in every
framework, and an update to it reaches all of them at once.

## Its own box

The viewer draws inside its own [shadow DOM](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Web_components/Using_shadow_DOM),
so your page and the viewer can't change each other by accident:

- Your page's CSS doesn't reach inside: not a global `button { … }` rule, not a CSS reset, not
  `html { font-size: 62.5% }`, not your `body` font or line height. The viewer sets its own sizes
  in pixels and its own text styles, so it looks the same on every page.
- The viewer's CSS doesn't reach outside, so it never changes your page.
- What you aim at it does reach it: its [CSS variables](../customize/theming#from-css) and its
  parts, through `::part()`.

Everything that belongs to the viewer stays inside its box: panels, sheets and dialogs, so a
dialog in a viewer that fills half your page covers that half, not your page. Menus and tooltips
may reach past its edge, like a menu in any app. They open in the browser's top layer, so they're
never hidden behind your page's own headers, and no `z-index` on your page is needed.

### Full screen

**Full Screen** (`view:fullscreen`) fills the screen with the viewer's box, not your page. The
viewer stays where your page put it, and nothing on your page moves or changes, however the
elements around it are styled.

- **In a frame**, the frame needs `allow="fullscreen"`: `<iframe src="…" allow="fullscreen">`.
  Without it the browser lets nothing in the frame go full screen, so the viewer has no Full
  Screen there.
- **On an iPhone**, where Safari has full screen only for video, the viewer covers the whole
  window instead, until the button or Escape ends it. A viewer that already is the whole window
  has no Full Screen there.
- **From your own code**, `requestFullscreen()` on the `<embedpdf-viewer>` element does the same,
  and the button shows it. On an element of yours around the viewer, with your own header say,
  the viewer's Full Screen still fills the screen with just the viewer, and ending it goes back to
  yours. To make the button do that, give `view:fullscreen` your own `run`: see
  [Change what a built-in button does](../customize/commands#change-what-a-built-in-button-does).

## Your components stay yours

When you put [your own component](../customize/components) in the viewer, it isn't moved into the
viewer. It stays where your framework rendered it, as a child of the `<embedpdf-viewer>` element,
and the browser shows it in the place the viewer made for it, through a `<slot>`.

That's why it keeps working the way you wrote it:

- your framework renders and updates it, with your providers, your router, your store;
- your CSS styles it, and the viewer's doesn't;
- its events reach your handlers as usual.

It does inherit the viewer's font, text color and CSS variables, so a plain element looks at home
without any styling.

## Plugins underneath

Under the interface are the [headless plugins](../code/plugins): stage, render, search,
annotation, form and the rest. Every button runs a command, every command talks to a plugin, and
the `viewer` gives you the same plugins. The interface holds no state of its own that you can't
reach.

## The engine

The PDF engine is PDFium, compiled to WebAssembly. It runs in a Web Worker, so a heavy page never
freezes your app, and its files come from your own build or your own server. With CloudPDF, the
engine runs on your server instead, and pages arrive as images.

## What loads when

The toolbar shows at once, while the engine starts in the background, and the pages show as soon as
the document is open. Every feature's plugin starts with the viewer, so the annotation tools are
ready for the first click.

The features' panels and dialogs, such as search, comments, stamps or the signature dialogs, aren't
part of that first load. Once the document shows, the viewer fetches them in the background while
the browser is idle, one at a time, so each opens at once; one opened before then loads right
away. A few heavier parts wait until they're used:

- the signature checks, until a signature is checked;
- the standard stamps, until Stamps opens;
- the handwriting fonts, until someone types a signature;
- each language, until it's chosen.

## Good to know

### React, Vue, Svelte, Angular

Inside, the viewer is built with Preact and ships as one finished file, so it doesn't use your
framework's copy and doesn't care which version you have. The wrapper for your framework only
passes your config in and your components through.

### Server-side rendering

On the server, the wrapper renders an empty `<embedpdf-viewer>` element of the size you gave it. The
viewer starts in the browser, when the page loads. See [Setup](../setup#server-side-rendering).

### Accessibility

Bars are toolbars to a screen reader, and arrow keys move between their buttons. Menus are menus,
panels are labeled regions, and dialogs trap focus until they close. Every button has a label from
its command, in the user's language.

# Your own annotation look

The annotation layer draws each annotation the way the PDF describes it. You can draw some of them
with your own <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> instead: a stamp that shows live data from your server, a note that looks
like your app's comment bubble, a highlight with a badge.

This changes the screen only. The PDF keeps the annotation's own look, so other PDF apps,
printing and downloads show the original:

| Where                               | What people see                 |
| :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| Your viewer                         | Your <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> |
| The saved PDF, other apps, printing | The annotation's own appearance |

<Snippet name="annotations/rendering-renderers" />

<Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>`for`</Fw><Fw only="angular">The function you give `epdfAnnotation`</Fw>
gets the annotation, with all its fields, so you can pick by kind, name, author or anything else.<Fw only="angular">
A subtype picks every annotation of that kind: `epdfAnnotation="stamp"`.</Fw> The first renderer that
matches wins; an annotation no renderer matches is drawn as usual.

<Fw only="react">

Define the list once, outside your component or in `useMemo`. The layer registers each entry, and
a new list every render would register them again (the viewer warns about it while you develop).

</Fw>

<Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>Your component gets:</Fw><Fw only="angular">Your template gets these:
the annotation is `let-annotation`, and the rest are named, such as `let-box="box"`.</Fw>

| Prop          | What it is                                                                          |
| :------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `annotation`  | The annotation, as `get()` returns it.                                              |
| `box`         | Where to draw it, in page coordinates. It follows a drag or a resize as it happens. |
| `page`        | The page: `page.transform.pageToViewRect(box)` turns `box` into pixels.             |
| `native`      | The layer's own drawing of it. Render it to keep the original look and add to it.   |
| `appearance`  | The engine's picture of the annotation (`url`, `box`), or `null`.                   |
| `hovered`     | True while the pointer is over it.                                                  |
| `interactive` | True when what you draw takes the pointer (see [below](#take-over-the-pointer)).    |

<Snippet name="annotations/rendering-badge" />

People can still select, move, resize and delete the annotation: your <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> draws, and the
viewer keeps handling the pointer. That's also why it gets no clicks of its own. For that, see
[Take over the pointer](#take-over-the-pointer).

## Show a card on hover

A hover card sits next to an annotation while the pointer is over it. It needs two things: the
annotation under the pointer, and an anchor that keeps a card attached to it:

<Snippet name="annotations/rendering-hover-card" />

<Fw only="react">Mount it in the Stage's `overlay`</Fw><Fw only="vue">Mount it in the Stage's `#overlay`
slot</Fw><Fw only="svelte">Mount it in the Stage's `overlay` snippet</Fw><Fw only="angular">Put it inside
`<epdf-stage>`</Fw>, like a menu. The card stays next to the annotation while the user scrolls and
zooms, and while the annotation is moved.

`useAnnotationAnchor(ref)` works for any annotation, not only the hovered one. Use it for a badge
that's always there, such as a count of replies next to each note.

## Example: an interactive stamp

A stamp that's an ordinary stamp in the PDF, but on screen shows its approval status, a card
with details on hover, and working buttons when you're reading:

<Snippet name="annotations/rendering-stamp" />

1. The stamp is placed like any stamp, so the PDF holds the real one; see [Stamps](stamps).
2. <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>`for`</Fw><Fw only="angular">`epdfAnnotation`</Fw> picks it out by its `name`, which the stamp keeps in every PDF app.
3. The <Fw only={['react', 'vue', 'svelte']}>component</Fw><Fw only="angular">template</Fw> draws the stamp itself (`native`) and your status on top.
4. In reading mode (the `pan` tool) the button works. With the `pointer` tool, people select and
   move the stamp as usual.
5. For a card on hover, add the [hover card](#show-a-card-on-hover) too.

To find your own data for an annotation, key it by `annotationKey(annotation.ref)`: the ref stays
the same after the document is saved and opened again.

## Good to know

### Hover cards people can use

A card closes when the pointer leaves the annotation, so the pointer can't reach a button in it.
Keep the card open while the pointer is over the card itself, for example with a short delay
before it closes and a `pointerenter` on the card that cancels it.

### Form fields

Form fields are annotations too. The `<FormLayer>` draws the fields it handles, and always wins over
your renderers for them; see [Forms and annotations](../forms#forms-and-annotations).

### Other layers

A renderer replaces the drawing inside the annotation layer. Anything that isn't tied to one
annotation, such as a comments sidebar, belongs in your own UI, fed by `useAnnotationList()`.

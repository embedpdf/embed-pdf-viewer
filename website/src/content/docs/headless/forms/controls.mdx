# Your own form controls

The form layer draws each field as an HTML control: a text box, a checkbox, a list. You can put your
own UI on a field, or draw its control yourself: a tag on the fields still to fill in, a hint next
to a field, a switch instead of a checkbox.

This changes the screen only. What people enter is still the field's value in the PDF, so other PDF
apps, printing and downloads show the field as the PDF describes it.

## Its control, or UI on it

Ask whether it should zoom and turn with the PDF:

| You want | Use | It |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A hint, a menu or a picker next to a field | [Anchored UI](../ui/anchored#attach-to-a-form-field) | Floats over the pages, and moves to stay in view |
| A tag that stays on a field: "Required", a signer's name, a ✓ | [Pinned anchored UI](#pin-a-tag-to-a-field) | Floats over the pages, upright and the same size at every zoom, exactly where you put it |
| A different control: a switch for a checkbox, a button inside a text box | A [control](#draw-a-control-your-way) | Is part of the page: it zooms and turns with the field |

It's the same choice as for [an annotation](../annotations/custom-rendering#its-look-or-ui-on-it).
Most of what an app adds to a form is the first two, and they need nothing from the form layer.

A field can show in several boxes, so an anchor is for one box: `form.getWidgets(field.ref)` lists
a field's boxes, each with its `ref`. Anchored UI on a page that isn't on screen isn't drawn, so a
form with hundreds of fields costs only the ones in view. See
[Where it goes](../ui/anchored#where-it-goes) for `placement` and `gap`.

A pinned tag keeps its size and its side at every zoom, and isn't moved to where there is room. The
room beside a field shrinks with the page: next to a field at the edge of a page shown small, such
as on a phone, there are a few dozen pixels. So keep a tag there small, as the example's is, or put
it on a side of the field that has room.

### The cursor and Tab

[Tab, `focus()` and `focusNext()`](../forms#move-between-fields) put the cursor in the first
element of your control that takes focus: an `<input>`, a `<button>`, or an element with
`tabindex="0"`. Give it one, and the field takes part in the form's order like any other. While the
cursor is anywhere inside your control, the field is the focused field. An element that is hidden,
disabled or inert doesn't count. A control with no element that does takes no cursor: Tab,
`focus()` and `focusNext()` pass over its field.

A box that is `disabled` takes no input: draw it, and leave out what people would click.

## What a box tells you

Each box says where it is, what it holds, and whose it is. `listWidgets(page)` gives the boxes of
a page, and a control gets its box as `widget`:

| Value         | What it is                                                                                         |
| :------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `control`     | Which control it is: `'text'`, `'toggle'`, `'choice'`, `'button'` or `'signature'`                 |
| `fieldRef`    | The field it belongs to: what `setValue()` and `get()` take                                         |
| `ref`         | The box's own ref: what `useFormWidgetAnchor()` and `getWidget()` take                              |
| `label`       | Its name for screen readers: the field's tooltip, else its name, then whose field it is when its [group](../forms#fields-for-several-people) has a label |
| `groupId`     | The [group](/docs/engine/forms/groups) the field is in, or `null`                                  |
| `canFill`     | Whether this user may fill it in, or sign it for a signature field: their permissions alone        |
| `accent`      | What the layer shows it in, `{ color, strength }`, or `null`: its [group's](../forms#fields-for-several-people) color and the `fields.tint` setting, while this user may fill it in and it still takes a value |
| `disabled`    | Draw it, take no input: a read-only field, one this user may not fill in, one [`fillable`](../forms#settings) leaves out, or a value on its way |
| `required`    | The form asks for a value                                                                          |
| `empty`       | It holds nothing yet: no text, nothing ticked or picked, no signature. What [`validate()`](../forms#check-required-fields) counts as missing |
| `box`, `rotation`, `rect` | Where it is; see [Turned fields](../forms#turned-fields)                               |

Then each control has its value: a text box its `value`, a checkbox or radio button `checked` and
its `exportValue`, a dropdown or list its `options` and `selected`, a signature box `signed`.

## Good to know

### Design mode

Controls fill forms in. They show while the active tool fills forms, as the layer's own do. In
[design mode](building#design-mode) fields are boxes people select and move, drawn by the
annotation layer, and your controls stand down with the layer's own.

### A group's color

A field in a [group](../forms#fields-for-several-people) shows in its group's color while its
user may fill it in. That color is part of the layer's own control, so a control that keeps
`native` shows it, and one that draws the box alone doesn't. `widget.accent` is what the layer
would show, `{ color, strength }`, or `null`, for a look of your own. A lent field has the same
`accent` on the annotation layer in design mode.

### Signature fields

An empty signature field's control is a button. A click makes the field the
[target](signatures#sign-here-in-a-form), where the next mark goes. Your own control for it calls
`signature.setTarget(widget.fieldRef)`, or opens your own signing UI.

### Dropdown lists

The list a dropdown opens is the browser's own, so it opens unturned and at the browser's size. For
a list in your app's look, draw the dropdown as a control and open your list as anchored UI.

### Drawing every field yourself

A control replaces one box inside the form layer, which still places it, turns it, and moves the
cursor between fields. To draw a form without the layer, `listWidgets(page)` gives you every box
with these same values.
